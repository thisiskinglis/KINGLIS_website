The Short Version —

A six-week craft-based programme that gives learners practical tools to name feelings, settle their bodies and cope in class, run on your campus with all materials supplied and minimal workload for staff.

  • Safety first: a named safeguarding contact, supervision and a referral pathway are agreed in writing before the first session.
  • Who: Grades 1-3 and Grades 4-7, in separate groups of 8 to 10 learners.
  • What: 6 weekly sessions of 45-60 minutes, fitted to your timetable.
  • Where: Your campus, during school hours or aftercare
  • Start: One pilot group in Term 1 2027
  • Cost: R2,250 per learner, materials included
  • Decision needed: Approval of one pilot group and a named safeguarding contact
The Problem Your Teachers Already See —

Children who cannot name or settle big feelings find it hard to learn, and teachers are often left to manage that alone. Teachers describe anxious learners, big feelings that spill into lessons, difficulty settling after transitions, and children who cannot say what is wrong. Many of these learners do not need a referral. They need skills, practised in a calm setting, that they can then use at their desk.

Talking-based support can be hard for these children. Making something with their hands lowers the pressure to speak, which is why this programme works through clay first and conversation second.

What Learners Gain —
  • Emotional vocabulary: words and shapes for feelings, and awareness of body signals
  • Calming skills: breathing, pause-and-reset and sensory regulation they can use in class
  • A personal calm kit: a tool each child builds and keeps
  • Confidence: strengths-based projects focused on what they can do
  • Belonging: SEN-informed adaptations so anxious, sensitive, neurodiverse and high-needs learners can take part without pressure to finish, explain or display work
The Six Weeks: Term 1 Foundations —

Foundations is the first programme in a termly series. Each later term is a new six-week programme that builds on it, so learners continue to grow and nobody repeats what they have done. Every session follows the same predictable rhythm: welcome and feelings check-in, creative invitation, making time, optional reflection and close.

How We Will Know It Is Working —

The pilot is designed to be judged on evidence, not promises. Before and after the six weeks we will gather:

  1. Attendance and participation across all sessions
  2. A short teacher observation checklist (settling after transitions, use of coping language, willingness to join in), completed in Week 1 and Week 6
  3. Learner feedback in age-appropriate form (faces scale or simple choices)
  4. Access adjustments used, so the school sees what inclusion looked like in practice

You receive a one-page programme summary after Week 6. Results are reported at group level only, with no individual clinical reports.

Safeguarding, Consent and Scope —

Before launch, agreed in writing: a named safeguarding contact, staff supervision arrangements, emergency response, incident documentation, and a referral pathway to the school counsellor or a named HPCSA-registered psychologist.

Consent and privacy: written parent or guardian consent and age-appropriate learner assent; plain-language information sheet; withdrawal at any time; no photographs of artwork without separate permission; POPIA-compliant secure storage; information shared only with consent or where safeguarding or legal duties apply.

Participation: learners may observe, pause, choose an alternative activity or opt out. Nothing has to be finished, explained or displayed.

What this is, and is not: art-based psychosocial wellness support that complements your pastoral, counselling and learner-support structures. It is not diagnosis, assessment, psychotherapy, counselling, hypnosis or HPCSA arts therapy, and it does not replace psychological services. Artwork is creative expression only and is never interpreted as diagnostic evidence.
